Professional Self-Assessment
Daniel Kleiner | Computer Science | Southern New Hampshire University

These last few years have been quite challenging to say the least.  I started back to school during the pandemic not long after my son was born, so the last few years have been extremely challenging and yet equally rewarding.  During this time, I was offered promotions at work and was still able to keep my GPA above 3.0 most of the time.  This did make learning some of the more difficult topics more difficult than I would have liked, there were many late nights a few repeated classes along the way, but I have been able to preserve and battle my way to the finish line.  Even though I have reached this finish line I realize that it is another starting line and I will continue learning as I hopefully will be able to make my career change.

During my time at SNHU we did not have that many opportunities to collaborate with our classmates directly, though the discussion posts were nice I have been collaborating as part of a team with most of my professional career.  I currently lead a team of 13 across two separate shifts and if we do not work together to get things done.  I have been able to use some of the things I have learned about agile methodologies and system analysis to help my team improve and work more effectively.

I have always been one who likes to teach and share my passions with others.  While I was here, I learned better ways of writing to convey technical subjects to a non-technical audience.  I’ve learned that communication is probably as important, if not more than, than many of the other skills that I have acquired while here.  I want to continue to learn and develop these skills as I would like to take a leadership role after I transition into my career.

One of the more difficult subjects I faced problems was Data Structures and Algorithms.  I struggled here especially with some of the prerequisite courses, but I now know the importance of calculus and linear algebra.  I have learned that many things in our world can be solved by figuring out the right algorithm for the problem and that not all data structures are created equally and that sometimes we need to figure out our priorities when we are selecting what solution we need.  
The software engineering and database course work were what I enjoyed the most, but this is also the area where I feel that I have the most opportunity for growth. While I learned a lot, I also feel that I have so much more that I can learn.  I have already been trying to figure out how to improve upon what I have accomplished during my formal studies, and I plan to continue to sharpen my skills that I have acquired while I search for work.  I know that while I like designing systems I have always struggled when I try to integrate different parts of the systems together. 

One of the places I find great interest in is security. I have always had an interest in engineering of all kinds; security is a point where software can fail and the outcomes can be catastrophic. I liked searching through code and looking for possible points of failure and then tinkering with the code to see if I can make it fail.  I was able to go to DEFCON and Black Hat while I was attending school and have learned that it is a deep field in the already deep field of computer science and that there is always something to learn no matter where you are.

When I was selecting my artifacts for the ePortfolio I tried to find projects that had a solid base that I could try to turn into an actual application that someone could use.  That is why I used the reverse engineering project for two of them, I took what I thought was a solid foundation and then developed it further.

The first artifact that I was to address software design and engineering. I took what I had accomplished in Software Reverse Engineering and then further refined the project making it more modular and easier to maintain.  My goal here was to just give the software better bones so that I could build further on it later.

The second artifact was to address data structure and algorithms.  This artifact was built initially with only one data structure, which I chose a hash map for the class.  Here I just rebuilt the artifact using other data structures.  I took a bit of time to see which the most common data structures were, but this is also modular so that if I want to experiment with other data structures, I can add new ones to the application as I see fit.

The third artifact addressed databases. I took the application from software design and engineering and then attempted to take out everything that was hardcoded into the application and build a database into it.  I struggled with this a bit, I was comfortable with SQL enough, but I took some time to learn how to use SQLite. To do this I used a combination of following the documentation, Stack Overflow, and YouTube videos.

These three artifact enhancements show how far I have come and the potential that exists within me. It shows that I am an engineer that will put in the work to find the solution with whatever tools that are available to me.  I may not be as experienced as many developers; I do feel that I could persevere and continue to follow my curiosities to continue to grow as developers.
